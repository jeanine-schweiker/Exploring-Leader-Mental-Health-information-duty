2. What is personal data?

Personal data is any information that directly or indirectly can be attributed to you. This can, for example, be your name and your age, but it can also be a lot of information that, when linked to other information, tells something about you, for example, your address combined with information from a public register.

3. Why do we process personal data about you?

As a university in Denmark, we have an obligation to conduct research. To conduct research, we often need to process personal data. In the research project "The causes and consequences of leaders' health disclosure at the workplace," we process personal data to better understand why organizational leaders disclose or conceal a health-related vulnerability to followers, peers and their own supervisors, and what consequences follow.

4. What information do we process about you?

We will process the following categories of personal data about you:

  • General personal data: your name, age, gender, contact details, workplace affiliation, job title, hierarchical rank and other background information you provide in connection with the project
  • As the project concerns health disclosure at the workplace, sensitive personal data related to your previous or current mental or physical health information will be processed.

How will we collect the information?

  • We collect this information directly from you, via interviews, conversations, correspondence, and observations carried out as part of the research project
  • The data is obtained only from you (as the individual data subject) and not through any third party.

5. What is the legal basis for processing your personal data?

The processing of personal data requires that there is a legal basis for the processing in the data protection rules or other legislation.

We process your personal data based on article 6(1)(a) of the General Data Protection Regulation, as you have given your consent to participate in the research project.

As the project involves the processing of special categories of personal data, this is processed based on Article 9(2)(a) in conjunction with Article 6(1)(a) of the General Data Protection Regulation.

You have the right to withdraw your consent at any time. If you withdraw your consent, Aarhus University will stop processing your personal data. To withdraw your consent, you can contact PhD fellow Jeanine Schweiker (see contact information above).

6. How long do we process your personal data?

We will, as a general rule, process your personal data for the duration of the PhD project and for up to two years after its completion, corresponding to a total storage period of up to five years.

This retention period indicates that the data may remain necessary not only for completing the PhD project but also for subsequent research activities. This includes preparing academic publications, potentially continuing the research in a postdoctoral context, teaching and knowledge exchange activities (e.g., workshops and presentations), and broader dissemination of findings through public communication (e.g., reports, debate articles, and op-eds).

In addition, the data may be required for documentation and verification of the research findings in accordance with the principles of responsible research practice and applicable legal requirements.

When your personal data is no longer necessary for these purposes, it will be anonymised or deleted.

When assessing how long it is necessary to retain your personal data, we take into account the need to fulfil the research purpose, ensure proper dissemination of results across academic, educational, and public contexts, and document the validity and integrity of the research for a period after completion.

7. Who do we share your information with?

Your personal data will primarily be processed by the researcher responsible for the project at Aarhus University.

Where relevant, your personal data may be shared with relevant researchers affiliated with the project, including supervisors and co-authors, for the purposes of conducting, analysing, and disseminating the research. In such cases, the data will, as far as possible, be shared in pseudonymised or anonymised form, and access will be limited to what is necessary.

Your personal data may also be shared with data processors acting on behalf of Aarhus University, for example in connection with secure storage or transcription services.

In connection with research collaboration or research stays at other universities, your personal data may be transferred to collaborating researchers or institutions, where this is necessary for the research project. Such transfers will take place in accordance with applicable data protection rules.

To ensure continuity of research when researchers change institutions/workplace, your personal data may be disclosed to other research institutions in Denmark or EU. This will only be done when appropriate data agreements are in place.

We do not share your personal data with others unless this is necessary for the research project or required by law.

8. What are your rights?

According to the General Data Protection Regulation, you have a number of rights when we process your personal data. Your rights are limited when the processing is carried out for research purposes. Therefore, you can read below what rights you have in connection with the processing of your personal data in research.

Rights in research

  • You have the right to have your data erased if the erasure of your data does not make it impossible or seriously impede the achievement of the research purpose.
  • In the event that we erase your personal data, we are obliged to notify any recipients of your personal data of the deletion, unless this proves impossible or is disproportionately difficult.

You can assert your rights by contacting the researcher: Jeanine Schweiker (see contact information on first page).

10. If you change your mind

Giving your consent is entirely voluntary. It is your choice, and you should therefore read this document carefully before deciding whether you wish to participate.

You may withdraw your consent at any time. You can do this by contacting PhD fellow Jeanine Schweiker (see contact information on p.1).

If you withdraw your consent, we will stop processing your personal data. However, you should know that the processing we have carried out up to the time when you withdraw your consent is lawful. You should also be aware that in rare cases your personal data will continue to be processed, even if you withdraw your consent. This applies, for example, to published material.
